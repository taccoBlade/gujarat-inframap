Top Tourist Destinations in Gujarat

  • Statue of Unity & Kevadia Complex (Narmada District): At 182 metres, the world's tallest statue stands on Sadhu Bet island overlooking the Sardar Sarovar Dam on the Narmada River. Dedicated to Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, the complex includes a viewing gallery at 153 metres, museum, Valley of Flowers, and riverfront promenade.
  • Rani ki Vav (Queen's Stepwell) (Patan District): Built in the 11th century CE by Queen Udayamati as a memorial to King Bhimdev I of the Chaulukya dynasty, this subterranean water management architecture is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Designed as an inverted temple highlighting the sanctity of water, it features seven pillared terraces with over 500 principal sculptures.
  • Somnath Jyotirlinga Temple (Gir Somnath District): Standing on the rugged shores of the Arabian Sea at Prabhas Patan, Somnath is reverently considered the first ('Prathama') among the twelve holy Jyotirlinga shrines of Lord Shiva. Rebuilt in the Chalukya Kailas Mahameru Prasad style under Sardar Patel's leadership post-independence, the temple grounds feature the famous Baan Stambh (arrow pillar) marking an unobstructed ocean meridian to Antarctica.
  • Sun Temple, Modhera (Mehsana District): Built in 1026 CE during the reign of King Bhimdev I of the Chaulukya dynasty on the banks of the Pushpavati River, the Sun Temple at Modhera is an architectural wonder designed so that the sun's rays at the equinox illuminated the inner sanctum. The complex comprises the Gudhamandapa (shrine hall), Sabhamandapa (assembly hall with 52 intricately carved pillars representing weeks of the year), and the magnificent stepped Surya Kund.
  • White Rann of Kutch & Dhordo (Kachchh District): The Great Rann of Kutch is one of the world's largest seasonal salt deserts, transforming during winter into an ethereal, blinding white expanse of crystal salts under cobalt blue skies. Best experienced during full-moon nights when the salt crust glimmers silver, the area hosts the world-famous Rann Utsav festival centered at Dhordo village, celebrating Kutchi music, dance, craft, and desert hospitality.
  • Dwarkadhish Temple (Jagat Mandir) (Devbhumi Dwarka District): One of the sacred Char Dham pilgrimage destinations in Hinduism, the 5-storey Jagat Mandir stands proudly on the Gomti creek near the Arabian Sea. Believed to have been originally established over 2,200 years ago by Vajranabh, great-grandson of Lord Krishna, the present limestone shrine rises 78 metres on 72 ornate pillars, flying the revered 52-yard triangular flag (Dhwaja) that is changed five times daily.
  • Gir National Park & Wildlife Sanctuary (Junagadh District): Covering 1,412 km² of dry deciduous teak and acacia forest, Gir is the only natural habitat of the endangered Asiatic Lion (Panthera leo persica) on Earth. Rescued from the brink of extinction by the Nawabs of Junagadh and subsequent conservation programmes, the sanctuary is also home to leopards, spotted deer (chital), sambar, nilgai, marsh crocodiles (mugger), and over 300 bird species.
  • Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park (Panchmahal District): A UNESCO World Heritage site since 2004, Champaner-Pavagadh represents a concentration of largely unexcavated archaeological, historic, and living cultural heritage. Set around the volcanic Pavagadh hill, it encompasses prehistoric chalcolithic sites, an 8th-to-14th-century Hindu hill fortress, and the 15th-century capital of Sultan Mahmud Begada displaying early Mughal and Indo-Islamic architecture with exquisite water-harvesting cisterns.
  • Sabarmati Ashram (Gandhi Ashram) (Ahmedabad District): Established in 1917 on the tranquil banks of the Sabarmati River by Mahatma Gandhi, this ashram served as the nerve centre of India's freedom struggle for 13 years. It was from here on March 12, 1930, that Gandhi led the historic 241-mile Dandi Salt March, vowing not to return to the ashram until India won independence.
  • Dholavira: A Harappan City (Kachchh District): Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021, Dholavira is one of the most remarkable and well-preserved urban settlements of the South Asian Bronze Age (3000–1500 BCE). Situated on Khadir Bet island in the Great Rann, it features an elaborate multi-tiered stone fortification, grand ceremonial grounds, a ten-symbol monumental Indus script signboard, and the ancient world's most sophisticated rainwater harvesting and storage system comprising 16 interconnected reservoirs carved into bedrock.
  • Polo Forest & Ancient Idar Valley Ruins (Sabarkantha District): Tucked away in the foothills of the Aravalli range along the perennial Harnav River, Polo Forest is an ancient hidden sanctuary where nature and medieval stone heritage intertwine. Established around the 10th century CE by the Parihar kings of Idar, the dense teak forest conceals ornate ruined Jain and Shiva temples, stepwells, and watchtowers overgrown with banyan roots.
  • Dada Harir (Bai Harir Sultani) Stepwell (Ahmedabad District): Built in 1499 CE by Bai Harir Sultani, a superintendent of the royal harem under Sultan Mahmud Begada, this five-storey sandstone stepwell in Asarwa is one of Ahmedabad's most magnificent yet quiet historic monuments. Unlike the heavily visited Adalaj stepwell, Dada Harir offers an unhurried experience of exquisite Indo-Islamic stone carvings, Sanskrit and Arabic dual inscriptions, and subterranean spiral staircases.
  • Lakhpat Walled Fort & Deserted Port Town (Kachchh District): Perched at the extreme northwestern tip of India where the Indus River once emptied into the Arabian Sea before the devastating 1819 Allah Bund earthquake changed its course, Lakhpat was once a bustling maritime trade hub generating over 100,000 koris in daily tax revenue (giving it the name Lakhpat). Today, it is an evocative, windswept deserted fortified town enclosed by 7 kilometres of towering battlements overlooking the Great Rann.
  • Khambhalida Buddhist Rock-Cut Caves (Rajkot District): Carved into soft limestone cliffs along the banks of a quiet seasonal spring near Gondal, the Khambhalida Caves date back to the 4th–5th century CE. Discovered in 1958 by renowned archaeologist P.P. Pandya, the central chaitya cave is flanked by two monumental, life-sized carved Bodhisattvas — Padmapani and Vajrapani — a rare and significant sculpture style in western Indian Buddhist art.
  • Galteshwar Ancient Shiva Temple (Kheda District): Standing serenely at the picturesque confluence of the sacred Mahi and Galti rivers near Dakor, Galteshwar is an 11th–12th century Solanki-Chaulukya stone temple. Dedicated to Lord Shiva, the temple features an unusual octagonal sanctum (Garbhagriha) and an exquisitely carved open assembly mandapa supported by eight pillars displaying figures of gods, apsaras, and musicians.

Frequently Asked Questions About Gujarat Tourism

What are the best places to visit in Gujarat?

The top places to visit in Gujarat include the Statue of Unity in Kevadia (world's tallest monument), Rani ki Vav stepwell in Patan (UNESCO World Heritage Site), Somnath and Dwarkadhish Jyotirlinga and Char Dham temples, Gir National Park (only home of wild Asiatic lions), the White Desert of Great Rann of Kutch, Modhera Sun Temple, and the Harappan city of Dholavira.

What is the best time to visit Gujarat?

The best time to visit Gujarat is between October and March when the weather is pleasantly cool and dry. This window coincides with the world-famous Rann Utsav in Kutch, migratory bird arrivals at Nal Sarovar, and optimal conditions for wildlife safaris at Gir National Park.

How to plan a trip to the Statue of Unity?

The Statue of Unity is located at Kevadia (Ekta Nagar), approximately 90 km from Vadodara. Tickets must be booked in advance online at the official portal (statueofunity.in). It is closed on Mondays. The complex features a 153m high viewing gallery, Valley of Flowers, evening projection mapping show, and electric bus network.

Which UNESCO World Heritage Sites are in Gujarat?

Gujarat hosts 4 UNESCO World Heritage Sites: Rani ki Vav (Queen's Stepwell) in Patan (inscribed 2014), Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park (inscribed 2004), Historic City of Ahmedabad (India's first World Heritage City, inscribed 2017), and Dholavira: A Harappan City in Kutch (inscribed 2021).

How many districts are in Gujarat?

Gujarat is administratively organized into 34 districts across Saurashtra, Kutch, North Gujarat, Central Gujarat, and South Gujarat. You can explore all 34 districts and collect territorial passport stamps on the Gujarat Pravas Atlas.
